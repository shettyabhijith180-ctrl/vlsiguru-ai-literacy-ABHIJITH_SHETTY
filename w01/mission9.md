# Mission 9 — Build My Own AI-Use Rule

## My Five Rules for Using AI in Engineering Learning

As a VLSI student, I can use AI to understand technical concepts, analyse problems, explore optimisation techniques, and improve my engineering skills. However, I must use it responsibly and verify important information before applying it.

### Rule 1: Verify Important Technical Information

I will not blindly trust AI-generated technical explanations, equations, calculations, or suggested solutions. I will verify important claims using reliable documentation, textbooks, technical references, or experiments before applying them.

**Example:** If AI suggests a method to fix a setup violation, I will check the timing constraints and verify the results after implementing the fix.

### Rule 2: Protect Confidential and Proprietary Information

I will not upload confidential company information, proprietary netlists, internal design files, restricted timing reports, or other sensitive engineering data to AI tools unless I have explicit authorisation to do so.

**Example:** When asking AI for help with Tcl scripting or timing analysis, I will use generic examples or appropriately sanitised data instead of sharing confidential design information.

### Rule 3: Take Responsibility for My Final Work

I will take responsibility for the engineering work I submit or implement, even when AI has helped me prepare it. I will understand the solution, review the output, and perform the necessary checks before using it.

**Example:** If AI generates a Tcl script for multi-VT swapping, I will inspect the script, understand its operations, and verify the resulting timing and power reports before accepting the changes.

### Rule 4: Understand Before Applying AI Suggestions

I will use AI to improve my understanding rather than simply copying its answers. Before applying a suggested solution, I will make sure I understand the underlying engineering concepts and the possible consequences.

**Example:** Before replacing an RVT or LVT cell with an HVT cell, I will understand how the change may affect leakage power, cell delay, and timing slack.

### Rule 5: Treat AI as an Assistant, Not an Infallible Authority

I will treat AI-generated answers as suggestions that may contain mistakes, missing details, or incorrect assumptions. I will question uncertain explanations and compare important claims against reliable sources or practical results.

**Justification from Missions 4 and 5:** In Mission 4, I investigated how an LLM generates an answer and checked important claims using technical documentation. In Mission 5, I verified the AI's explanation of setup time against official Intel and AMD documentation. Although the explanation was correct, this investigation taught me that an answer should not be accepted simply because it sounds convincing. These missions showed me the importance of checking AI-generated technical information against reliable sources before applying it in engineering work.

## My AI-Use Agreement

I will use AI to support my engineering learning, protect confidential information, verify important technical claims, understand the solutions I apply, and take responsibility for my final work.

> My goal is not to trust AI blindly or reject it completely, but to know when and how to verify its output.
